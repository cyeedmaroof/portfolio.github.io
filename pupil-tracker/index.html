What do these numbers mean?
Pupil/iris ratio is the pupil diameter divided by the iris diameter — independent of how far you sit from the camera. Dilation % compares each image's average ratio to your calibrated baseline. Positive = pupils larger than baseline (engagement/arousal or a darker image), negative = constricted (often a brighter image). Img brightness is the average luminance of the image: because the pupillary light reflex is strong, big brightness differences between images can drown out emotional responses — compare images of similar brightness for the most meaningful results.
Honest caveats. This is a fun prototype, not a medical or research instrument. Webcam pupil estimation is noisy and works best with good even lighting, a close face, and a decent camera. The pupil's reaction to screen brightness is far stronger than its reaction to emotion, so keep image brightness similar and treat differences cautiously. Brown eyes are harder (low pupil/iris contrast) — check the live overlay shows the magenta circle sitting on your actual pupil before trusting the numbers.
